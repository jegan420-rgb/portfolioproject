SERIF FONTS

Serif fonts have small “feet” at the ends of letter strokes. Serif fonts offer a high degree of readability in longer written works like books and academic textbooks.

SANS SERIF FONTS

Sans serif font types, as the name states, lack serifs and offer a cleaner, more minimalist aesthetic. Sans serif fonts are well-suited for digital screens and can also serve as headings when the body of the text is in a serif typeface.

SCRIPT FONTS

Script font types mimic handwriting or calligraphy, notable for their fluid strokes throughout the lettering. Typically used for invitations or any other scenario when an elegant touch is called for.

DISPLAY FONTS

Display fonts are stylized to command attention from the reader, and feature prominently in headlines in magazines and on websites, logos and related branding, as well as posters and packaging. Since display fonts are used to grab attention, they’re not suited for use in body text or in paragraphs.

MONOSPACED FONTS

Monospaced fonts are characters that occupy the same horizontal width. Monospaced fonts were originally intended for use with typewriters but are now prominently used in programming (coding) apps because of their excellent uniformity.
